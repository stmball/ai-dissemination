What is this activity?

In this workshop you will use n8n — a free, visual automation tool that works entirely in your web browser — to build a workflow that reads research papers sent to your email inbox and automatically writes a plain-English summary of each one. A lay summary is a short, jargon-free explanation of a research paper written for a general audience, not just specialists. You do not need to write any code. Think of n8n as a series of connected steps: each step does one job, and together they run automatically, one after another.

💡
What you'll build A workflow that watches your Gmail inbox, reads a PDF research paper attached to an incoming email, and automatically replies with a plain-English lay summary of the paper.

Your workflow — single paper lay summary:

📬Gmail Trigger
Step 1 Watch for an incoming email The workflow starts when a new email arrives in your inbox. The trigger also downloads any PDF attachments automatically.
📄Extract Text
Step 2 Pull the text out of the PDF The PDF is opened and its text is copied out so the AI can read it.
🤖AI Summary
Step 3 Write a plain-English lay summary The AI reads the paper and writes a short, jargon-free summary.
↩️Reply
Step 4 Reply with the summary The lay summary is sent back as an email reply to the original message.

Getting set up

Before building your workflow, you need a free n8n account and a Google account to use for sending and receiving emails. Go to n8n.io and sign up for a free trial. There is nothing to download or install — everything runs in your browser.

1

Go to n8n.io, click Get started free, and create an account. Once you are signed in you should see a blank screen with the message "Add first step" — that means you are ready to build.

2

Set up a Google account for this activity

The workflow you are about to build will watch a Gmail inbox for new emails, read any PDF attachments, and send an automated reply with the summaries. You need a Google account to make this work. You can use an existing Google account or create a new one at accounts.google.com — a new account is a good idea if you would rather keep this activity separate from your personal email.

Either way, make a note of the Gmail address. You will send your research paper to this address, and the workflow will automatically reply with the summary.

03 —

The activity: Reply with a lay summary

In this activity you will build a workflow that watches your Gmail inbox for a new email with a research paper attached, reads the PDF, and automatically replies to the sender with a plain-English lay summary of the paper.

💡
How it works You send an email to your Google account with one PDF attached. The workflow detects the new email, extracts the text from the PDF, asks the AI to write a plain-English summary, and replies to your email automatically.
1

Watch for incoming emails and download the attachment

Click the + button on the n8n canvas and search for "Gmail Trigger". Select it and choose your saved Gmail credential from the dropdown. Set Event to Message Received.

Then, uncheck "Simplify", click Add option and add Download Attachments — turn this on. Also add the Attachment Prefix option and set it to attachment_. This tells the trigger to save any PDF files attached to the incoming email so the next step can read them.

2

Extract the text from the PDF

Click + and search for "Extract from File". Set the operation to Extract Document Text and set the Input Binary Field to attachment_0.

"Input Binary Field" is just n8n's name for the attached file it should read. The value attachment_0 refers to the first attachment — the prefix you set in step 1, with a zero added on the end to indicate the first file. This step opens the PDF and converts its contents to plain text so the AI can read it, similar to copying and pasting text out of a document.

3

Ask the AI to write a lay summary

Click + and search for "OpenAI". Set the operation to Message a Model and choose your saved credential. Set the Model to GPT-5.4-NANO In the User Message field, write your instruction — for example:

"Write a lay summary of the following research paper in plain English, suitable for a general audience with no specialist knowledge. Use 3–4 sentences. Paper text: {{ $json.text }}"

The {{ $json.text }} part is a placeholder that n8n replaces with the actual extracted paper text from the previous step.

4

Reply to the original email

Click + and search for "Gmail". Select it, choose your credential, and set the operation to Reply to Message.

For the Message ID field, you need to tell n8n which email to reply to. Click inside the field, then look at the left-hand panel — it shows the data produced by each previous step. Find the Gmail Trigger entry, expand it, and drag the id value into the field. n8n will fill in the expression for you automatically. Then set the Message field to:

{{ $json.output[0].content[0].text }}

This tells n8n to insert the AI's summary as the body of the reply.

✅
Test it: Send an email to your Google account with one PDF research paper attached. Press Test workflow in n8n and watch each step light up. After a few seconds you should receive a reply containing a plain-English summary of the paper.
04 —

Running & testing your workflow

Before running everything at once, it is worth testing each step individually to catch any problems early.

1

Test each step on its own

Click any step on the screen and press "Test step" (the play button inside the settings panel). n8n will run just that step and show you the result on the right-hand side. Check it looks right before moving on to the next one.

2

Run the whole workflow

Press the "Test workflow" button at the bottom of the screen. You'll be able to watch each step light up as it runs. If a step turns red, click on it to see the error message — most problems are caused by a typo in a field or a credential that hasn't been connected yet.

3

Check your inbox for the reply

If the Gmail reply step shows a green tick, the reply has been sent. Open your inbox and check the reply has arrived — it should contain a short plain-English summary of your paper. If the summary looks wrong or is missing, click the OpenAI step in your workflow to see exactly what the AI produced, and check the prompt you typed in.

✅
Check the run history After running, go to Executions in the left-hand menu to see a record of everything that happened. Click on any run to see the result at each step — useful if a summary came out blank or the reply failed to send.

What else could you do with n8n?

The workflow you have just built is a small example of what automation can do. The same principles — watch for something, do something with it, send a result somewhere — can be applied to almost anything that involves information moving between systems. Here are a few ideas pitched directly at nursing practice and study.

💡
These are just ideas to spark your imagination. You do not need to build any of these today. Each one could be put together using the same approach you used in the activity — a trigger, a few processing steps, and an action at the end.
💬

Get a Slack or Teams message when a new guideline is published

Many professional bodies and NHS trusts publish updates via RSS feeds — a simple web feed that lists new content as it appears. You could build a workflow that checks a feed once a day and posts a short summary of any new guidance directly to a team Slack channel or Microsoft Teams group, so nobody has to remember to check manually.

📅

Send yourself a weekly reading digest

Rather than reacting to individual emails, you could set a workflow to run every Monday morning. It could pull together a handful of recent papers from a database or journal feed, summarise each one, and email you a neatly formatted digest — ready to read over your morning coffee before the week begins.

📋

Log every summary to a shared spreadsheet

You could extend today's workflow to also write each summary to a Google Sheet alongside the paper title, date, and topic. Over time this builds a searchable record of all the research you have reviewed — useful for reflective portfolios, evidence logs, or just keeping track of what you have read.

🔔

Alert your team when a patient-relevant keyword appears in new research

You could set up a workflow that scans new publications for specific terms — a drug name, a condition, a procedure your ward specialises in — and sends a notification to a shared inbox or channel only when something relevant is found. Instead of reading everything, your team only hears about research that matters to them.

🌐

Translate a summary into another language

n8n can connect to translation services as easily as it connects to AI. A small addition to today's workflow could take the lay summary and translate it into any language — useful for sharing research findings with colleagues, patients, or families whose first language is not English.
